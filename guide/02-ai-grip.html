02 · AI Grip Size ​

Feature 1 of 4. Snap a photo of your palm → recommended grip size (Yonex L0–L5 / Wilson in fractions / Head–Prince–Babolat equivalent).

What it does ​

Hold the phone up to your palm. The App estimates the following on-device by default (no upload):

  • Palm length (mm)
  • Palm width (mm)
  • Finger count + ratios (calibration across hand shapes)
  • → Recommended grip size in three systems simultaneously

How to take an accurate photo ​

Rule 1: lay the hand flat on an A4 sheet on a table ​

Yes, the same image doubles as the adjust-screen shot — the layout (palm up, fingers spread, on a paper outline) is exactly what you want before capture. Take the photo in roughly this pose and the AI's first guess will be very close to your hand's true anatomy.

  • Flat surface — lay an A4 sheet of plain paper on a hard, flat table. Don't shoot in your palm or against a soft surface.
  • Palm flat on the paper, fingers spread — your skin's contact with the paper gives the AI a crisp edge to anchor on. The A4 outline also normalises the camera distance (matches roughly a 30 cm working distance).
  • Palm up (face the camera) — back-of-the-hand photos have very little texture for MediaPipe to anchor; palm-up photos are far more reliable.
  • Don't curl the fingers — fingers must be extended and spaced apart, even if it feels slightly strained.

Rule 2: even lighting ​

   ✅ Good               ❌ Bad
┌────────────┐     ┌────────────┐
│  white wall│     │  messy desk │
│   🖐       │     │   🖐 + 📦   │
│            │     │            │
└────────────┘     └────────────┘
  • Light should be even and diffuse; sidelight through a window is better than a harsh ceiling lamp
  • Turn off the flash if the room has enough ambient light
  • A4 paper diffuses harsh overhead light nicely — another reason to use it

Rule 3: phone parallel to the hand ​

  • Don't shoot top-down / from below — straight overhead
  • Camera ≈ 30–50 cm above the paper
  • Use the rear camera (front camera has heavy distortion)

Two ways to correct the AI ​

Before walking through what the result screen looks like, here's the two adjust modes the App offers — pick whichever fits the situation:

Mode 1 — Fine-tune on the result page (preferred) ​

This mode lets you drag the wrist crease line on the photo. The wrist crease is where the AI estimates the start of the palm; if it's 5 mm too high (toward the fingers), the recommended size jumps up.

  • After capture, you see your hand with a horizontal line drawn at the AI's best guess for the wrist crease
  • Long-press and drag vertically to move the line
  • "Measurements" + "Sizes" panels update live as you drag
  • Drag range is limited to ±15 mm from the AI's initial guess (prevents wild swings)
  • When you're happy, Save to history

This is the mode you should use when the AI's number is in the right ballpark but slightly off (typically a 3–10 mm correction).

Mode 2 — Adjust Green Frame, align the A4 paper to the green box ​

If the overall scale of the result is off — e.g. you wore the wrong overgrip earlier and the photo is shrunken or stretched — switch to the adjust screen to give the App a known physical reference.

The screen is titled "Adjust Green Frame" and shows:

  • Your full photo (palm + A4 paper)
  • A teal-green rectangular frame the App computes at the camera's expected working distance (~30 cm overhead)
  • A bottom-bar hint: "Drag with one finger; pinch to zoom (rotation disabled). Fit the A4 inside the green frame, then Next."
  • A green Next (Analyze) button

What to do:

  • Drag with one finger to translate the photo so the A4 paper is centered under the frame
  • Pinch with two fingers to scale the photo so the A4 paper's four edges line up with the green frame
  • Try to get the four paper edges flush with the green frame — the closer they are, the more accurate the scale calibration
  • Rotation is disabled (so you don't have to worry about accidentally tilting the photo)
  • Tap Next (Analyze) to re-run the palm detection with the corrected scale

Because an A4 sheet has a known physical size (210 × 297 mm), telling the App "the green frame = an A4 sheet at this distance" pins down the absolute scale of the photo. This fixes overall-size errors that Mode 1 (line drag) can't reach.

Combine the two: Mode 2 for scale, Mode 1 for the start of the palm.

Photo flow ​

  1. Home → AI grip size
  2. Start measurement → request camera permission (first time)
  3. Hold the phone overhead (≈ 30–50 cm above the paper). Aim at the laid-flat palm. Hold still for 1 second for pose pre-check.
  4. Capture (or auto-trigger)
  5. Wait 1–2 seconds: on-device MediaPipe Hand detection → result screen

Result ​

The result screen shows the AI's palm detection overlaid on your photo, side by side with the numbers:

PanelContent
Photo + overlayYour hand with the wrist crease line (the horizontal line where the palm meets the wrist) drawn by the AI
MeasurementsPalm length + width in mm
SizesYonex / Wilson / Head-Prince-Babolat equivalents

The three-system grid reads:

                  Yonex   Wilson    Head / Prince / Babolat
                  L3      4½"       4¾

If the line is off, use Mode 1 (drag the wrist crease line) — see Two ways to correct the AI above.

If the line isn't there or the numbers look obviously wrong, tap Adjust scale only → Mode 2 adjusts.

Error budget & mitigations ​

Error sourceImpactMitigation
Tilted palm±5–8 mm in lengthApp prompts "place flat"
Lens distortion (front camera especially)±3 mmDefault uses the rear camera
Skin tone / lighting±2 mm keypoint driftMediaPipe Hand 2024 model is robust across skin tones
Hand occlusion (ring, long nails)failure / size overshootRemove rings; trim nails first
Hand-shape training bias±3 mmUse Mode 1 (drag the crease line) or Mode 2 (adjust screen slider)

RMSE in tests: on-device 4.2 mm. Translated to size = about ±⅓ size in the Yonex system.

Limits & boundaries ​

  • ❌ Gloves not supported: gloves wipe out the keypoints
  • ❌ Children's hands: training data is mainly adults; child hands are unreliable
  • ❌ Extreme lighting (very dark, strong backlight): capture fails → use manual adjust
  • ❌ Multi-person frames: App picks the largest palm (still error-prone)

Original photos are not saved ​

V1.1 does not keep the original photo. After the measurement, only the keypoint coordinate array (~80 bytes) is cached for the history view. If you need "keep the photo for later recompute", enable it under Settings → Data → Keep original photos (off by default).

Common questions ​

Does iPhone / iPad work? ​

This version does not support iOS. See 00 · Platforms.

How much compute does on-device need? ​

Recommended Snapdragon 7xx / Dimensity 800 or above. Low-end phones may spin for a few seconds.

Does my measurement data get uploaded? ​

No. Everything runs on-device; the image never leaves your phone.

I disagree with the AI's number — what now? ​

Two options, in order of fidelity:

  1. Fine-tune on the result page — long-press the wrist crease line and drag it vertically; the measurements + sizes update live. Use this when the AI's number is in the right ballpark but slightly off (typically a 3–10 mm correction).
  2. Adjust screen — tap Adjust scale only on the result page → single ±10 mm slider. Use this when you already measured with a ruler and just want to type the number in, or when on-device detection failed entirely and you never saw the photo.
